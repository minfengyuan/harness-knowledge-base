# Storm Research

Use independent perspectives when a question benefits from adversarial comparison, broad evidence, or hidden connections. Choose the number and roles from the question and available capacity; a fixed multi-agent ritual is not required.

## Prepare

- Establish the question, scope, time boundary, source standard, and decision the research should support.
- Infer safe defaults. Ask only when ambiguity would materially change the research.
- Choose perspectives that cover genuinely relevant operational, scholarly, critical, economic, historical, or domain-specific angles.
- Prefer primary and authoritative sources, verify time-sensitive claims, and distinguish fact, source-reported view, and inference.

## Investigate

- Run independent searches in parallel when that materially improves speed or coverage; use sequential research when the question is narrow or concurrency adds little.
- Give each researcher a focused scope and require evidence, counterevidence, limitations, and unresolved questions when those are relevant to the assigned angle.
- Compare agreements, contradictions, source authority, directness, recency, and independent corroboration before synthesizing.
- Never invent citations. If browsing or a source is unavailable, state the gap plainly.

## Report

Answer the question first. Then present the findings, evidence strength, disagreements, recommendations, confidence, or research gaps that materially help the user's decision. Do not force a fixed ranking, confidence label, or section when the evidence or task does not benefit from it. Label inferences and do not force consensus where evidence remains mixed.
